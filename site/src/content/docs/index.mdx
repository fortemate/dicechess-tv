<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Family chess with a roll of the dice" icon="random">
    Three dice choose which pieces may move each turn. Luck gives a newcomer a
    real chance against a stronger player, so a family can play one game
    together.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Made for the remote" icon="rocket">
    The arrows jump between the pieces that can move, then between the chosen
    piece's destinations. Over 200 simulated games, a turn takes about 9 presses
    instead of 23 square by square.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Three opponents on the TV" icon="puzzle">
    Rolly plays any legal turn at random, Grabby takes the most valuable piece
    it can, and Rampage goes for your king. You can play either colour: Play as
    in Settings draws a colour at random, names White or Black, or asks before
    each game.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Music that follows the game" icon="seti:audio">
    Four themes by pepka-prygni: one for the menus, and three that grow tenser
    as your king comes into danger. Settings sets the music's volume, and turns
    the sound effects and the voices on or off.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Offline and private" icon="seti:lock">
    The rules, the computer opponents and the saved games all stay on the TV.
    There is no account, and the app's source contains no network calls.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Rules anyone can read" icon="open-book">
    The app's code is licensed under AGPL-3.0-only, and it plays by Fortemate's
    open-source rules engine, so anyone can read how a move is judged.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

The TV app was built during [Build, Ship, Shape: Amazon Developer Hackathon 2026](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/), in a repository started on 21 September 2026. Version 1.0.0 was released on GitHub on 10 October 2026 and submitted to the Amazon Appstore, where it awaits Amazon's review. It plays by the rules of Fortemate's open-source engine, [`@fortemate/dicechess-engine`](https://github.com/fortemate/dicechess-engine), which existed before. The [friction log](/friction-log/) records the obstacles we met in Amazon's Vega SDK along the way.
